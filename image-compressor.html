Free Image Compressor: Reduce Photo Size in KB Online

Large photos are a daily problem. Job portals say "upload photo under 50 KB", exam forms want a signature below 20 KB, and WhatsApp or email often struggles with big pictures. This free image compressor reduces the file size of your JPG, PNG and WebP images right in your browser. You pick the quality, choose a maximum width, and download the smaller copy. Your pictures are never uploaded.

How to compress an image

  1. Press the file box and choose one or more images from your phone or computer.
  2. Move the quality slider. Lower quality gives a smaller file.
  3. Choose JPG or WebP as the output format.
  4. Select a maximum width if you want to shrink the picture dimensions too.
  5. Press Compress images. Each result shows the old size, the new size and the percentage saved.
  6. Press Download next to each image.

How compression works

Photos hold far more detail than the eye needs. Compression throws away small details that are hard to notice and stores the rest more efficiently. This is called lossy compression. At 70 percent quality, a photo usually looks nearly identical but may be five to ten times smaller. At very low quality you will see blocky patches and blurry edges, so check the preview before you use the file.

How to reduce an image to 100 KB, 50 KB or 20 KB

Start with quality at 70 percent and a maximum width of 1280 px. If the file is still too big, lower the quality in steps of 10 and compress again. For very small targets like 20 KB, choose 800 px width as well. Reducing the width helps more than reducing quality alone, because fewer pixels need to be stored. Always compress from your original photo and not from an already compressed copy, as repeated compression lowers quality each time.

JPG or WebP: which format should you choose?

JPG works everywhere, including government portals, email and printing shops. Choose JPG when you are not sure. WebP usually gives a smaller file at the same quality and is ideal for websites and blogs, but some older forms and software do not accept it. For uploading to Indian portals, JPG is the safest choice.

What happens to PNG images?

PNG files are large because they keep every pixel exactly. They are good for logos and screenshots but poor for photos. This tool converts PNG into JPG or WebP, which makes the file much smaller. One thing to know: transparent areas become white, because JPG has no transparency. If you need a transparent background, keep the PNG.

Why compress images?

Smaller images upload faster on slow mobile networks, fit within portal limits, save phone storage and use less data. If you run a website, compressed images load faster, which improves visitor experience and can help search ranking. Online shops and bloggers often compress every picture before publishing.

Tips for good results

  • Do not go below 50 percent quality for photos with faces or text.
  • Use a smaller width for pictures that will only be seen on a phone screen.
  • Check the downloaded image before uploading it to a form.
  • Keep your original photo safe in case you need a better copy later.

Is it private?

Yes. The compression happens on your own device using your browser. Your photos, including ID photos and documents, are not sent to HisaabKitab or any other server, and nothing is saved when you close the page.

Compressing photos for forms, WhatsApp and websites

Different uses need different sizes. For job and exam forms, check the stated limit and compress until you are just under it, because a smaller limit does not mean you need the smallest file possible. For WhatsApp and email, 1280 px width and 70 percent quality keeps pictures sharp on a phone while making them light to send. For a website or blog, use WebP at around 75 percent quality and a width close to how large the image appears on the page. A banner rarely needs more than 1920 px. Compressing before you upload saves data, speeds up your pages and keeps storage use low.

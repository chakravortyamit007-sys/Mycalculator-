BMI categories

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Normal weight
25 – 29.9Overweight
30 and aboveObese

These are the standard WHO categories. Some Asian health guidelines use lower cut-offs (for example, overweight from 23), so check with your doctor.

How BMI is calculated

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m) × height (m).

Example: A person who is 170 cm tall and weighs 65 kg has a height of 1.70 m. BMI = 65 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 65 ÷ 2.89 = 22.5, which is in the normal range.

For imperial units the formula is BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)².

Limits of BMI

BMI is a quick screening number. It does not measure body fat directly and does not separate muscle from fat, so athletes and very muscular people may show a high BMI while being healthy. It is also less accurate for children, pregnant women and older adults. Use it as a guide and talk to a doctor for health advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered the normal range.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. It is a general screening tool and can be misleading for athletes, older adults, pregnant women and children.

How can I lower my BMI?

A balanced diet, regular physical activity, good sleep and gradual weight change are the usual approaches. A doctor or dietitian can give a plan that suits you.

How do I convert feet and inches to cm?

Multiply feet by 30.48 and inches by 2.54, then add them. For example, 5 ft 7 in = 152.4 + 17.78 = 170.2 cm.

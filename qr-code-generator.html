What is a QR code?

QR stands for Quick Response. It is a square pattern of black and white modules that stores text. When a phone camera reads it, the phone decodes the text and decides what to do. A web address opens in the browser, a UPI link opens a payment app, and a WhatsApp link opens a chat. The codes made here are static, which means the information is stored inside the picture itself. They never expire and do not depend on our website to keep working.

Types of QR code you can make

Website link: paste any address, such as your shop page or Instagram profile. If you forget https://, the tool adds it. WhatsApp chat: enter a 10-digit Indian number and an optional message. When customers scan, WhatsApp opens a chat with you and the message is already typed. This is popular for orders, enquiries and bookings. UPI payment: enter your UPI ID, your name and, if you like, an amount. Apps such as Google Pay, PhonePe and Paytm open a payment screen with your details filled in. Plain text: store an address, a note or any short message.

Making a UPI QR code for your shop

Many small businesses print a UPI QR code and keep it at the counter. To make one, enter your UPI ID exactly as it appears in your payment app, for example name@bank. Add your shop name so customers see it before paying. Leave the amount empty if customers will type their own, or enter a fixed amount for a set price such as a membership fee. Before printing, scan your code with your phone and make a small test payment to your own account. Check that the name and UPI ID are correct on the payment screen.

Making a WhatsApp QR code for orders and enquiries

A WhatsApp QR code removes the need to save a number before chatting. Cafes can put it on menu cards, tutors on pamphlets and shops on windows. Add a short message like "Hi, I want to order" so you know why the customer is writing. Keep the message simple, because long messages make the code denser and harder to scan.

Size, colours and error correction

Size changes the number of pixels in the PNG. Use 300 px for screens and 800 px when printing on large boards, so edges stay sharp. For colours, always keep strong contrast. A black code on white is the safest. Avoid light colours on a light background and avoid inverted codes, because some phones cannot read them. Error correction decides how much damage the code can take and still scan. Low is the smallest pattern. Medium suits most uses. Quartile and High add redundancy for printed codes that may get dirty or scratched, but the pattern becomes denser.

Tips for printing QR codes

  • Print at least 2 cm by 2 cm. Larger is better for codes that people scan from a distance.
  • Leave a blank margin around the code. Do not let text or pictures touch it.
  • Use matte paper or a matte laminate. Glossy surfaces reflect light and block scanning.
  • Test with two or three different phones before printing many copies.

Is it safe to use?

Your link, phone number, UPI ID and text are processed in your browser. They are not sent to our server or saved. When you close the page, the data is gone, so download your PNG first. Only share QR codes that you created yourself, and be careful when scanning codes from unknown people, because a code can hide any link.

Will my QR code stop working?

No. Static codes keep working forever. If you change your link or number, make a new code.

Why will my QR code not scan?

Usually the contrast is low, the code is too small or the text is too long. Use dark on light, increase the size and shorten the content.
